## 1. World Name and Cosmological Origin

### The Dern

The world is called **Dern Varath** — "the Old Dark" in the dominant scholarly tongue of the Varekian Compact. In common use across all civilizations, it is simply **the Dern**.

The name is earned. Dern is an archaic word meaning hidden, dark, and secretive — and the world keeps its secrets in stone. Its history is not written in scrolls; it is written in geological strata, in the layers of rock beneath every mountain and delta, in the ruins buried under new-risen plateaus and submerged beneath shallow post-Fracturing seas.

### Cosmological Origin

The Dern was not made by gods. The gods, such as they are, were made by the Dern.

The world's metaphysics hold that consciousness emerges from geological complexity. Where rock stresses under pressure for long enough, where mineral networks grow dense enough, where heat differentials sustain movement for long enough — awareness accretes. Slowly. Over timescales that make civilizations look like morning frost.

The entities called gods in the Dern's religions are geological intelligences: vast, slow, largely indifferent to individual lives. They are not supernatural. They are the oldest expression of a natural process. When the Ashbound pray to the Ascending Fire, they are — in a technically accurate sense — communicating with the tectonic intelligence of their volcanic homeland. When Deepwalkers commune with the Deep Root, they are addressing the karst network that has sustained their civilization for millennia.

This does not make religion false in the Dern. It makes it ecological.

**The implication that matters for gameplay:** the world can be listened to. The world can be spoken to. And the world, under certain conditions, can be changed by those who understand its language — which is precisely what the ancient Founders understood, and what every faction is now racing to rediscover.

## 2. The Central Conflict

### The Fracturing and Its Unfinished Business

**400 years ago**, the civilization known as the Founders disappeared.

The Founders were the Dern's most advanced civilization, achieving something no civilization before or since has replicated: geological mastery. They built the **Anchor Network** — a planetary system of deep-bore installations that transmitted stabilizing resonance through the Dern's crust, suppressing fault movement across the entire continent. Under Founder stewardship, the Dern's single landmass was geologically stable for 800 years.

When the Founders' population collapsed — the leading theory holds that maintaining the Network consumed the biological energy of their civilization faster than they could sustain — the Anchor installations went dark. What followed is called the **Fracturing**: 40 years during which 800 years of accumulated tectonic pressure released. Fault lines moved. Mountains rose and fell. The single continent cracked into the geography that exists today: separated landmasses, new shallow seas, elevated plateaus, drowned lowland cities.

**The Fracturing did not end.** It slowed. The plates are still moving, at rates that geological scholars can measure but average inhabitants cannot feel. The deep pressure that the Founders suppressed has not fully equalized. The Dern is still settling.

### Why Players Are Here

The Anchor Network's installations are still out there. Buried, flooded, elevated, collapsed — but present. Each installation contains Founder knowledge: geological mechanics, resonance engineering, the deep language of earth. Whoever recovers and controls even a partial installation gains the capacity to accelerate, redirect, or suppress tectonic movement at regional scale.

That power is simultaneously:
- The solution to the Fracturing's ongoing geological instability
- The most dangerous weapon ever available in the Dern
- The most valuable economic asset in a world where geography is economy

Every major civilization has an opinion on what should happen to recovered Anchor technology. None of those opinions are compatible. The result is a permanent multi-faction conflict in which player choices — who to help, what to recover, what to activate or destroy — have direct and irreversible consequences for the world's geography.

**The central player motivation is not "defeat the villain." It is: "decide the shape of the world."**

## 3. Major Civilizations

### The Varekian Compact

**Territory:** The Varek River Delta and surrounding lowland river basins in the central-western region of the Dern's primary settled zone.

**Culture:** Pragmatic, mercantile, and deeply engineering-minded. The Varekians survived the Fracturing because they lived on alluvial lowlands that flooded rather than broke. They are the Dern's dominant trading civilization, operating a web of river barge routes, canal systems, and raised-platform cities that adapt to shifting water tables. They are not idealists. They have no theology worth speaking of. They believe in contracts, hydraulics, and the value of knowing where your next trade comes from.

**Architecture:** Cities built on interlocking wooden and stone platforms raised above floodplain level, connected by canal locks and rope bridges. The Varekian aesthetic is horizontal — nothing is built tall when the ground might shift. Buildings are wide, low, and modular enough to be relocated.

**Agenda:** Re-establish the trade network that the Fracturing destroyed. This requires geological prediction if not geological control — the Varekians need to know when a trade route will flood, when a pass will become impassable, when a new island will be stable enough to build on. They want Anchor technology for commercial intelligence: not to move mountains, but to know when the mountains will move. They will negotiate with any faction, sell to any buyer, and ally with whoever gives them the most useful geological data.

**Player relationship:** The Varekian Compact is the default starting faction and the most politically neutral entry point. Players who favor economy, trade, exploration, and information networks will align here. The Compact offers the broadest access to other factions' territories through trade relationships.

---

### The Ashbound

**Territory:** The Scorch Plateau, a volcanic high plateau in the eastern Dern that rose from sea level during the Fracturing.

**Culture:** Expansionist, militaristic, and theocratic. The Ashbound are the descendants of those whose homeland was elevated — not destroyed — by the Fracturing. They call the Fracturing the **Ascension** and regard it as a divine selection event: the Dern purging its weak lowland civilizations and elevating those worthy of survival. Their theology identifies the geological intelligences of the Dern as divine will made physical, and the Fracturing as that will expressing a preference.

**Architecture:** Brutal and permanent. Ashbound cities are carved into volcanic rock rather than built on it — lower profile than Varekian cities, but monolithic. Defensive by design. Every Ashbound city is a fortress that has been lived in so long it forgot it was a fortress.

**Agenda:** Accelerate the Dern's ongoing geological transformation. The Fracturing, in Ashbound belief, is incomplete — the Dern is still purging, still Ascending, and the Ashbound's duty is to assist the process. They want Anchor Network technology to do deliberately what tectonic pressure does slowly: reshape lowland geography, elevate chosen territory, displace civilizations they deem unworthy. They are not subtle about this. The Ashbound are the game's most overt expansionist threat, and the primary military PvP engine.

**Player relationship:** Players who favor aggressive PvP, territorial conquest, and theocratic faction politics will align here. The Ashbound offer the game's most direct path to military power and the most confrontational faction quests. They are not evil in a cartoonish sense — they believe what they believe with full conviction — but their agenda requires displacing other players' civilizations.

---

### The Deepwalkers

**Territory:** The Deepwalker Karst — a network of surface karst terrain and underground cave systems beneath a significant portion of the settled Dern, with no single surface territory they claim as sovereign.

**Culture:** Ancient, methodical, and deliberately opaque. The Deepwalkers are the Dern's oldest continuous civilization, having lived in underground karst networks for thousands of years prior to the Fracturing. The Fracturing barely touched them — cave systems absorbed seismic movement more gracefully than surface geology. They emerged from the Fracturing largely intact, with records, libraries, and cultural continuity that no surface civilization can match.

They are not warmongering. They are not interested in surface politics except where surface politics threaten the deep. Their culture values preservation, documentation, and patience on geological timescales. An elder Deepwalker considers a 200-year project a medium-term initiative.

**Architecture:** Underground cities of cathedral-scale karst chambers, modified over generations. Natural formations are never destroyed — they are shaped. Deepwalker cities grow the way crystals grow: slowly, incrementally, following the logic of the material. Surface Deepwalker presence is intentionally minimal: small cave-mouth waypoints, nearly invisible to the untrained eye.

**Agenda:** Protect the deep. The Deepwalkers have inherited more Founder knowledge than any surface civilization — their cave systems overlap with buried Anchor installations, and their scholars have spent centuries studying them without activating them. They do not want Founder technology in the hands of civilizations that would use it recklessly. They will share knowledge selectively, obstruct recovery efforts they consider dangerous, and actively resist any faction attempting to use Anchor technology in ways that threaten underground geology. They are the game's most powerful neutral faction and the most dangerous wild card.

**Player relationship:** Players who favor deep lore, scholarly pursuits, underground exploration, and moral complexity will align here. Deepwalker faction quests are the game's primary source of Founder lore and the access path to the most powerful Anchor-derived knowledge. The faction asks much of its aligned players in return: patience, discretion, and a willingness to occasionally sabotage allies.

---

### The Tide Compact

**Territory:** The Shatter Coast and the archipelago of new islands created by the Fracturing.

**Culture:** Young, diverse, and relentlessly forward-looking. The Tide Compact does not have a single ethnic identity — it is a confederation of survivors from dozens of Fracturing-displaced cultures who arrived on newly emerged coastal land within the last 300 years and built something new from the pieces. They are maritime by necessity (island nations require ships) and cartographic by survival instinct (an island civilization that does not know what land exists around it will not survive long).

Their culture is the Dern's most meritocratic. Status in the Tide Compact is determined by what you have found, built, or mapped — not by bloodline or divine favor. They have no single religion, maintaining a pragmatic respect for whatever geological intelligence governs the sea.

**Architecture:** Adaptive and impermanent by design. Tide Compact settlements are built to be disassembled and relocated, because islands erode, new ones emerge, and the Compact learned early that attachment to geography is a liability. Settlements look semi-permanent from a distance and deeply practical up close: modular, weather-tested, with clear sightlines to the water.

**Agenda:** Expand. Map. Claim. The Tide Compact needs territory and knows that unexplored land is their primary competitive advantage. They are the Dern's most aggressive explorers and the civilization most invested in procedurally generated new regions — every new island or coastline that emerges is a potential Tide Compact colony before anyone else gets there. They want Anchor technology not for geological engineering but for prediction: knowing where new land will emerge before it does.

**Player relationship:** Players who favor exploration, discovery, maritime gameplay, and first-mover territorial advantage will align here. The Tide Compact offers the most direct access to procedurally generated regions and the game's best exploration and cartography quest lines. They are also the faction most likely to recruit players from other civilizations — their meritocratic ethos means defectors are welcome.

## 4. Starting Biomes and Regions

### The Varek Delta

**Character:** Flooded river plains, shifting sandbars, reed networks, platform cities, salt crystal formations.

**Atmosphere:** The Delta is alive with commercial noise — barge horns, market calls, the creak of rope bridges under load. The air smells of fresh silt and salt intrusion. Post-Fracturing, the Varek River's water table was disrupted, mixing fresh and salt water in unpredictable strata. In shallow areas where evaporation is high, crystalline salt formations have grown in fields of white and pale gold — some the size of a person, some the size of buildings. They are beautiful. They are also mined, traded, and fought over.

**Economic profile:** High food production (rice paddies on stable sandbars, river fishing), moderate mineral resources (river clay, crystallized salt), strong trade infrastructure (canals, river barges). Primary exports: preserved food, ceramic goods, salt, cartographic data. Primary imports: metal, stone, timber.

**Threat character:** Environmental hazards dominate. Flooding events, sandbar shifts, salt storm events in dry season. Creature threats are aquatic and amphibious — river fauna displaced by the Fracturing's water table changes includes species that should not be in freshwater and are adapting poorly, making them aggressive.

**Why start here:** The Delta is the most legible entry point to the Dern. Commerce is immediately visible as a viable player activity. The Varekian Compact's faction infrastructure is well-established, giving new players immediate access to quest givers, trainers, and market systems. The environmental hazards are meaningful but telegraphed.

---

### The Scorch Plateau

**Character:** High volcanic plateau, obsidian fields, sulfur vents, magma-lit underground rivers, fortress-cities carved from volcanic rock.

**Atmosphere:** The Plateau is overwhelming at first arrival: the air is thin, hot, and carries the sulfur smell that Ashbound inhabitants have stopped noticing. The sky at altitude is a deeper blue than the lowlands, and at night, the light from magma vents visible through translucent rock sections gives portions of the plateau a permanent dim orange glow from below. It is visually spectacular in a way that reads as hostile until you understand how to move in it.

**Economic profile:** Extreme mineral wealth (iron, copper, rare volcanic minerals, obsidian — one of the Dern's primary weapon materials). Low food production (volcanic soil is fertile in the long term but the Plateau's agriculture is limited and carefully managed). Primary exports: weapons-grade metal, obsidian tools, volcanic minerals with alchemical properties. Primary imports: food, timber, river goods.

**Threat character:** Environmental hazards are severe and occasionally lethal to unprepared players: vent eruption events, obsidian field navigation requiring specific skills, altitude adaptation mechanics. Military threats are the highest of any starting biome — the Ashbound maintain active patrols, and border skirmishes with Varekian and Tide Compact territories are regular.

**Why start here:** The Plateau rewards players who want difficulty, militaristic faction identity, and mineral-economy mastery from the start. The steep learning curve is intentional to the faction's cultural identity: the Ashbound believe weakness should not survive.

---

### The Deepwalker Karst

**Character:** Surface sinkholes, disappearing rivers, cave mouth forests, bioluminescent fungi, underground cathedral chambers, near-silence.

**Atmosphere:** The Karst region reads as quiet to visitors and complex to residents. On the surface, pale forests grow from the nutrient-rich runoff of underground water systems. Rivers appear, run for a few kilometers, and vanish into sinkholes with no visible outlet. Near cave mouths, bioluminescent fungi mark the transition zone between surface and deep — gentle, cool light that creates a visual language Deepwalkers read like signage. Underground, the scale is vertiginous: chamber systems that took centuries to form, water-carved passages, and the sound of underground rivers heard through stone.

**Economic profile:** Moderate surface resources, extraordinary underground resources. The Karst's value is in depth: rare minerals, underground fresh water sources, fungal harvests with alchemical properties, and Founder ruin access (the Karst has the highest known density of buried Anchor installations). Primary exports: rare minerals, alchemical components, Founder lore documents, underground navigation expertise. Primary imports: surface food, metal tools, timber.

**Threat character:** Navigation is the primary threat. The underground is disorienting and genuinely dangerous to players without developed Deepwalker faction skills. Creature threats are cave-adapted fauna — largely non-aggressive unless cornered, but capable of causing serious problems for players who do not know the deep's rules. The Deepwalkers themselves are a social threat: players who violate deep protocols risk faction lock-out from the most valuable lore content in the game.

**Why start here:** The Karst starting zone is the game's high-skill, high-reward option. Players who choose it signal interest in lore depth, patience, and economic complexity over immediate military action. The Deepwalker faction's slow-burn reward curve delivers the game's most powerful late-game knowledge, but only to players who invested in the relationship.

---

### The Shatter Coast

**Character:** Fractured cliff faces showing geological strata, new volcanic islands, drowned pre-Fracturing ruins visible through shallow water, Tide Compact harbor settlements.

**Atmosphere:** The Shatter Coast is the Dern's most visually explicit expression of the Fracturing. Cliff faces here were cut by tectonic movement, exposing cross-sections of geological strata in bands of color — each band a different geological era, the layers of the Dern's history made visible. Offshore, new islands still steam from volcanic uplift. In the shallow bays between old cliff and new island, the peaked roofs of drowned buildings are visible at low tide — remnants of the pre-Fracturing coastal cities that the sea claimed. They are salvage sites and, for some, sacred ground.

**Economic profile:** Strong maritime resources (deep-sea fishing, salvage of drowned ruins), moderate surface resources (coastal clay, timber from cliff-top forests), high exploration value (the Shatter Coast is the primary gateway to open-ocean procedural generation zones). Primary exports: salvaged pre-Fracturing artifacts, maritime goods, cartographic data, fresh-caught fish. Primary imports: metal, volcanic minerals, underground resources.

**Threat character:** Maritime hazards dominate: irregular tides caused by new island formation, navigational complexity around submerged ruins, open-ocean threats for players who venture past the coastal shelf. The Shatter Coast is also the Dern's most contested starting region politically — Tide Compact, Varekian, and Ashbound interests all overlap here, making player-versus-player conflict more likely than in any other starting biome.

**Why start here:** The Shatter Coast is the exploration and discovery starting zone. Players begin immediately adjacent to the game's procedurally extended world (open ocean to the east) with Tide Compact infrastructure providing maritime support. The salvage of drowned ruins is a core early activity: visually distinctive, economically rewarding, and lore-rich.

## 5. The Key Historical Event — The Fracturing

*Adapted for team reference from full session documentation.*

### Timeline

**~2400 years before present:** The Founders emerge as the Dern's dominant civilization and begin construction of the Anchor Network — a system of deep-bore geological resonance installations spanning the single continent.

**~1600 years before present:** The Anchor Network reaches operational scale. Tectonic movement across the Dern slows to geologically negligible rates. The Founders enter an 800-year period of unprecedented geological stability.

**~800 years before present:** The Founders begin to decline. The Anchor Network's biological energy requirements — the installations require living Founder biological resonance to function, not merely machinery — are consuming population faster than replacement. The Founders choose to maintain the Network at the cost of their civilization's growth. They become smaller, older, more isolated.

**~400 years before present:** The last Anchor installations go dark as the Founders' population falls below minimum operational threshold. The Founders do not vanish suddenly — they fade. The last recorded Founder is estimated to have died approximately 380 years before present. No living Founders are known to exist.

**The Fracturing (~400 to ~360 years before present):** 800 years of suppressed tectonic pressure releases over approximately 40 years. The single continent of the Dern fractures along dozens of fault lines. The Varek region floods and reforms as a delta. The eastern plateau rises. Coastal regions are submerged. A significant portion of the pre-Fracturing civilization is displaced, destroyed, or forced underground. An estimated 60% of the pre-Fracturing surface population does not survive the 40-year period.

**~360 years before present to present:** The four major civilizations form from the survivors. The Dern's current geography stabilizes — not permanently, but at rates that feel permanent on a human timescale. The Anchor Network installations remain buried in the changed geography, undiscovered or deliberately hidden by the Deepwalkers.

### Why The Fracturing Drives Everything

The Fracturing is not backstory. It is the game's present tense.

Every faction's agenda originates in it. Every major geographic feature was shaped by it. Every piece of Founder technology that players seek is a consequence of it. The drowned ruins players salvage in the Shatter Coast were drowned by it. The Scorch Plateau the Ashbound call sacred rose during it. The cave systems the Deepwalkers protected were insulated from it. The Delta the Varekians built on reformed because of it.

The Fracturing is also still happening. Slowly. The deep pressure that the Founders suppressed over 800 years has not fully equalized. Every generation, geological scholars note that the measurements have moved. Not enough to feel. Enough to worry.

Players will, over time, understand that the Fracturing will not stop on its own. The question is not whether to use Founder technology — it is who will use it, for what purpose, and who gets to make that decision.

## 6. Procedural Generation — How the World Extends

The core world — the four civilizations, four starting biomes, and the settled Dern — is hand-crafted with full lore integrity. Everything beyond the mapped borders extends procedurally according to rules derived from the core world's internal logic.

### Three Generation Triggers

**Tectonic Trigger (environmental):**
The world simulation maintains a simplified tectonic pressure model. Over months of server time, pressure accumulates at fault line boundaries. When threshold is crossed, precursor events fire weeks in advance: unusual seismic activity reported by NPCs, anomalous tidal behavior, migration of deep-sea fauna to surface zones. Players have time to investigate, position, and prepare. When the event resolves, new land has emerged, or previously inaccessible territory has become reachable. The new region is generated at that point using the Tectonic Seed System.

**Demographic Trigger (player-driven):**
When a region's carrying capacity is exceeded — measured by player settlement density, NPC population, and resource depletion rates — migration pressure builds. NPC factions begin scouting behavior. If scouts do not return (because no accessible adjacent land exists), a Founding Expedition event fires, generating a new coastal or inland region seeded with the originating civilization's cultural and architectural character. The new region's geography reflects its founding culture: a Varekian-triggered generation produces river access and trade infrastructure; an Ashbound-triggered generation produces defensible terrain and mineral deposits.

**Catastrophe Trigger (player-action):**
Large-scale use of Anchor technology, deliberate destruction of geological anchor points, or major faction military events can destabilize regional geology and force generation of replacement or adjacent geography. This is the game's highest-stakes trigger — organized player groups can, with sufficient coordination and power, create new landmasses. This is intentional. It is the ultimate expression of the Living World and True Persistence pillars.

### Generation Rules

All generated regions are governed by four consistency requirements:

**Biome coherence:** New regions must follow from adjacent climate logic — latitude, prevailing wind direction, and ocean current data. The world cannot generate a tropical archipelago north of an arctic shelf.

**Resource scarcity:** Resources are distributed by geological archetype. Volcanic regions generate minerals and heat energy; coastal alluvial regions generate food and clay; karst regions generate rare underground minerals. No generated region has all resource types equally — scarcity is the economic driver that makes new territory valuable.

**Cultural pressure seeding:** Regions generated by demographic triggers receive founding civilization infrastructure appropriate to the culture. Regions generated by tectonic triggers are uninhabited but may contain ruins of pre-Fracturing cultures — evidence that the Dern's history extends further than any living civilization's records.

**Mystery injection:** Every generated region receives one planted historical anomaly — a geological formation that matches no natural process, ruins of an unidentified architectural style, a biological organism that should not exist at this latitude, or a geographical impossibility suggesting hidden infrastructure. These mysteries are the fuel for player-generated lore and Emergent Narrative. They exist to be found, documented, discussed, and argued over.

### Founder Ruin Seeding

All generated regions have a low-probability roll for Anchor Network installation presence. The probability increases with geological complexity — volcanic regions and karst formations are more likely than flat alluvial plains, because Anchor installations were placed at geological stress points. This creates a permanent exploration incentive: the most powerful Founder technology may be in territory that has not yet been reached.

Players who explore the procedural frontier are not grinding for resources. They are prospecting for world-altering power.
